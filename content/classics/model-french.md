Mikhail Botvinnik played the French for much of his career, and it served him well. This 1941 game against Igor Bondarevsky shows the Advance from Black's side. The c-pawn strikes at the base, the f-pawn strikes at the head, and an advanced pawn finishes the game.

Take Black from move three.

```game
side: b
from: 3
caption: Bondarevsky v. Botvinnik, Leningrad/Moscow 1941
moves: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.Nf3 Nc6 5.Bd3 cxd4 6.O-O Bc5 7.a3 Nge7 8.Nbd2 Ng6 9.Nb3 Bb6 10.Re1 Bd7 11.g3 f6 12.Bxg6+ hxg6 13.Qd3 Kf7 14.h4 Qg8 15.Bd2 Qh7 16.Bb4 g5 17.Qxh7 Rxh7 18.exf6 gxf6 19.hxg5 e5 20.gxf6 Kxf6 21.Bd6 Re8 22.Nh4 Rg8 23.Kh2 Bf5 24.Re2 d3 25.Rd2 dxc2 26.f4 Be3 27.Bxe5+ Nxe5 28.fxe5+ Ke7 29.Rf1 c1=Q
note: 3...c5 | The strike at the base of the chain, played at once.
note: 5...cxd4 | Black removes the pawn, and the d4-square is now under Black's control.
note: 11...f6 | The second lever. Now the head of the chain is under fire as well.
note: 19...e5 | Black has built a strong pawn centre of his own.
note: 24...d3 | The passed pawn begins its march.
note: 29...c1=Q | The pawn queens, the reward for an attack on the chain that began on move three.
```

Black's king stayed in the centre for most of the game and was never in danger. Once White's chain had been dismantled, the white pieces had no targets left to aim at.
