Bobby Fischer was thirteen when he played this game at the Rosenwald Memorial in New York in 1956. The journalist Hans Kmoch called it the Game of the Century. Fischer's queen sacrifice on move seventeen is the celebrated moment, but the moves leading to it are just as instructive.

Take Black from move eleven. A correct guess scores a point.

```game
side: b
from: 11
caption: Donald Byrne v. Robert Fischer, New York 1956
moves: 1.Nf3 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.d4 O-O 5.Bf4 d5 6.Qb3 dxc4 7.Qxc4 c6 8.e4 Nbd7 9.Rd1 Nb6 10.Qc5 Bg4 11.Bg5 Na4 12.Qa3 Nxc3 13.bxc3 Nxe4 14.Bxe7 Qb6 15.Bc4 Nxc3 16.Bc5 Rfe8+ 17.Kf1 Be6 18.Bxb6 Bxc4+ 19.Kg1 Ne2+ 20.Kf1 Nxd4+ 21.Kg1 Ne2+ 22.Kf1 Nc3+ 23.Kg1 axb6 24.Qb4 Ra4 25.Qxb6 Nxd1 26.h3 Rxa2 27.Kh2 Nxf2 28.Re1 Rxe1 29.Qd8+ Bf8 30.Nxe1 Bd5 31.Nf3 Ne4 32.Qb8 b5 33.h4 h5 34.Ne5 Kg7 35.Kg1 Bc5+ 36.Kf1 Ng3+ 37.Ke1 Bb4+ 38.Kd1 Bb3+ 39.Kc1 Ne2+ 40.Kb1 Nc3+ 41.Kc1 Rc2#
note: 11...Na4 | A startling move. The knight cannot be taken, because the queen on c5 would be left to Nxe4 tactics.
note: 13...Nxe4 | The centre pawn falls, and the white king is still in the middle.
note: 17...Be6 | Fischer leaves his queen to be taken.
note: 18...Bxc4+ | White's king is driven back, and the windmill begins.
note: 19...Ne2+ | A sequence of checks, each collecting more material.
note: 23...axb6 | The queen is more than repaid: Black has rook, two bishops and a pawn for it.
note: 41...Rc2# | Fischer's pieces have coordinated around the king to deliver mate.
```

When you finish, go back to move seventeen and consider what Black gets for the queen. Sacrifices at this level are not speculative. They rest on careful counting.
