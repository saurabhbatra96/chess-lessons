When White exchanges on d5 the resulting pawn structure has a name: the Carlsbad. White's plan is usually the minority attack, in which the a- and b-pawns advance to create a weakness on Black's queenside. Black's reply depends on piece activity and on a knight route to e4.

```line

note: 4...exd5 | The bishop on c8 is freed, the same gift as in the Exchange French.
note: 5...c6 | Reinforcing d5 and giving the queen the c7 square.
note: 8...Re8 | The rook takes the open file before any other piece is committed.
note: 10...Nf8 | The knight is heading to e6 or g6, and it guards h7.
```

## The Plans

Keep these three ideas in mind.

- **The e4 outpost.** With a knight on e4, Black gets active play and blocks the e-file. Put a piece there when you can support it.
- **Kingside chances.** White's pawns are on the queenside, so Black's rook on e8 and bishop on d6 can turn on the king.
- **Do not hurry to stop b4-b5.** When the pawn arrives, answering with ...cxb5 or leaving it alone can both be right. Look at which of your pawns would be left weak.
