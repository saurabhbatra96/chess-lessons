Plenty of opponents will not play the Queen's Gambit at all. The good news is that Black can set up with ...d5, ...e6, ...Nf6 and ...c5 against almost everything, so a very small amount of knowledge goes a long way.

## The London System

The bishop comes to f4 outside the pawn chain, and White builds a solid triangle. Black's best response is an early ...c5.

```line
side: b
caption: The London, met with ...c5
moves: 1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 Nf6 3.e3 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nd2 e6 6.Ngf3 Bd6 7.Bg3 O-O 8.Bd3 b6
note: 3...c5 | Challenging d4 at once. This is the most useful move against the London.
note: 6...Bd6 | Offering to exchange White's best piece.
note: 8...b6 | The problem bishop finds a home on b7, or a6.
```

## 1.c4 and 1.Nf3

```line
side: b
caption: 1.c4 e6, with a Catalan-style fianchetto
moves: 1.c4 e6 2.Nf3 d5 3.g3 Nf6 4.Bg2 Be7 5.O-O O-O 6.b3 c5
note: 1...e6 | The move that keeps you in your own structures.
note: 6...c5 | Black claims the centre, having completed development.
```

If White plays d4 at any point in these lines, you are back in a Queen's Gambit Declined. Look for that transposition and be glad of it.

## The Colle and Other Quiet Systems

Against 1.d4, 2.Nf3 and 3.e3 Black has an easy game. Develop and play ...c5 before White can get going with e4.

```line
side: b
caption: The Colle System
moves: 1.d4 d5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.e3 e6 4.Bd3 c5 5.c3 Nc6 6.Nbd2 Bd6
note: 4...c5 | The same central challenge as against the London.
note: 6...Bd6 | Black is at least equal, and already thinking about castling.
```
