Every tactic in this section has a victim, and you will often be that victim. Improvement at club level depends as much on the combinations you prevent as on the ones you play, and defensive puzzles are the ones that most players leave undone.

## Name the Threat

Before you can parry a threat, you must see it. So ask, after every opposing move: **what does that move want to do next?** If your opponent could play again right now, what would they play? This one question exposes most threats while there is still time.

## Look for the Active Defence

When several defences exist, prefer the one that does something else as well. A defence that also creates a threat can turn the initiative around. If you must be passive, choose the option that gives up the least, and do not despair: the attacker still has to find the win.

When things have gone badly, these resources are worth checking:

- A check or capture that disrupts the attack, since even a lost piece may buy decisive time.
- Simplifying into an ending that can be held, such as one with opposite-coloured bishops or a drawn rook ending.
- Stalemate, when you have very little left to move. Many winning positions have been thrown away by a careless final queen move.
