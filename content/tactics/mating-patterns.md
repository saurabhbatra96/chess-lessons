Strong players do not calculate every mate from first principles. They recognise a picture, then calculate only whether they can reach it. Each pattern below is worth learning as an image, the way you learned the shape of a word.

## Smothered Mate

The king is entombed by its own pieces, and a lone knight delivers the blow. Since no piece can capture or block a knight, its own army has become the king's coffin.

```board
fen: 6rk/5Npp/8/8/8/8/8/6K1 b - - 0 1
caption: The smothered mate. Rook and pawns take away every square, and nothing can reach the knight.
mate: yes
```

The classic route is known as Philidor's Legacy: a check from the knight drives the king to the corner, a queen is sacrificed on g8 to force the rook there, and the knight mates. You will meet it in the puzzles below.

## The Arabian Mate

One of the oldest patterns on record, with rook and knight in partnership against a cornered king.

```board
fen: 7k/7R/5N2/8/8/8/8/6K1 b - - 0 1
caption: The Arabian mate. The knight defends the rook and covers g8.
mate: yes
```

## Anastasia's Mate

A knight takes away the flight squares from a distance, and a rook finishes down the edge of the board, the king's own pawn barring its last escape.

```board
fen: 8/4N1pk/8/7R/8/8/8/6K1 b - - 0 1
caption: Anastasia's mate. The knight on e7 covers g8 and g6.
mate: yes
```

## Boden's Mate

Two bishops, crossing on diagonals, against a king castled long. Its namesake game shows how little material it takes once the diagonals open.

```board
fen: 2k1rb1r/ppp3pp/2n2q2/3B1b2/5P2/2P1BQ2/PP1N1P1P/2KR3R b - - 0 14
caption: Schulder v. Boden, London 1853. Black to play and mate in two.
best: f6c3
arrows: f6c3
```

Boden played 14...Qxc3+ 15.bxc3 Ba3, and the two bishops cover every flight square that White's own rook and knight have not already blocked. The queen was given away just to open a single diagonal.
