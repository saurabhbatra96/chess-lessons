We have spent ten lessons on forcing moves, and for good reason. Your list of checks, captures and threats will find most combinations. But the third item on that list is the hardest to see, because a quiet move gives no check and takes nothing. It only makes a threat so large that there is no adequate reply.

```board
fen: r4rk1/pp3p1p/6p1/4B1Q1/8/8/PP3PKP/8 w - - 0 1
caption: White to move and mate in two. There is no check available that helps.
best: g5f6
arrows: g5f6 f6g7
```

The queen steps quietly to f6, and the bishop on e5 stands behind it. Black now has a free move and all the time in the world, except that nothing stops Qg7 mate. The rooks cannot reach any useful square, and the king cannot run. Quiet moves work when the opponent has no forcing replies of their own, so always confirm that before you play one.

## Clearance

Sometimes your own piece is the only thing in your way. A clearance move vacates a square or a line, usually with tempo, so that another piece may use it. If you can see a beautiful move that is blocked by your own man, ask how that man can leave with a threat.

## Interference

Interference is the opposite: you place a piece on a line between two of your opponent's units so that they can no longer support one another. The piece you interpose may be captured, but the capture costs time or breaks the connection. Look for enemy pieces that defend one another along a long line, then look at the squares in between.

## Thinking in Quiet Moves

When the forcing moves all fail, do not give up the search at once. Ask what you would play if your opponent had to pass. That move, whatever it is, tells you what the position wants. Then see whether it can be played right now.
