## The Guard Is Overworked

In real games, the back rank is usually guarded by a piece or two. The combination lies in pulling that guard away, and it can be done by the plainest of means.

```board
fen: 2r3k1/p1q2ppp/8/8/8/2Q5/5PPP/4R1K1 w - - 0 1
caption: White to move. The rook on c8 defends the queen, and it also guards the back rank. It cannot do both.
best: c3c7
arrows: c3c7 e1e8
```

White simply takes the queen. If the rook recaptures, it has left the back rank, and Re8 is mate. So Black must accept being a queen down. The defender was defending in name only.

## Taking Out Insurance

The cure is cheap. At some quiet moment after castling, a pawn move such as h3 or g3 gives your king an escape square, which old writers called *luft*, or air. Do not make it too early, since tempi matter in the opening. But once the heavy pieces begin to face each other across open files, it is often the best quiet move on the board.

Check your own back rank before every combination you launch, and your opponent's before every move you make.
