The Advance is the most straightforward test of the French. White takes space with e5 and builds a pawn chain from b2 to e5. Black's answer is the theme of the whole defence: attack the chain at its base, which here is d4.

Everything Black does in the next few moves puts another piece on d4. The c-pawn hits it, the knight on c6 hits it, and the queen on b6 joins in, while also staring at b2.

```line
side: b
caption: The main line, with 6.Be2
moves: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.Be2 cxd4 7.cxd4 Nh6 8.Nc3 Nf5 9.Na4 Bb4+ 10.Bd2 Qa5
note: 3...c5 | The strike at the chain, played at once.
note: 5...Qb6 | The queen piles onto d4 and b2.
note: 7...Nh6 | The knight is bound for f5, its ideal square, where it attacks d4 once more.
note: 10...Qa5 | Black keeps active pieces, and d4 remains a long-term target.
```

## 6.a3 and the Queenside Expansion

White may prepare b4 to gain queenside space. Black continues in the same spirit.

```line
side: b
caption: 6.a3
moves: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.a3 Nh6 7.b4 cxd4 8.cxd4 Nf5 9.Bb2 Be7
note: 7...cxd4 | Clearing the c-file before White can consolidate.
note: 9...Be7 | Black prepares to castle, and later ...a5 undermines White's queenside.
```

## A Trap to Avoid

In the line with 6.Bd3, the d4-pawn looks as if it is simply hanging. It is not. If Black takes twice, White plays Bb5+ with a discovered attack on the queen.

```line
side: b
caption: 6.Bd3: develop first, then take
moves: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.Bd3 cxd4 7.cxd4 Bd7 8.O-O Nxd4 9.Nxd4 Qxd4 10.Nc3 a6
note: 7...Bd7 | Now Bb5+ is no longer a threat, so the pawn really is attacked.
note: 8...Nxd4 | Black can accept the Milner-Barry Gambit.
note: 10...a6 | Taking b5 from White's pieces. Black is a pawn up and must complete development carefully.
```
