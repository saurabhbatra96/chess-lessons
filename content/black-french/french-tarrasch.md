With 3.Nd2 White keeps the c-pawn free and avoids the pin that would follow 3.Nc3. Black's most direct reply is to hit the centre with 3...c5.

After the usual exchanges Black ends up with an isolated pawn on d5. Do not be alarmed. It controls e4 and c4, and it gives Black open lines and active pieces. In return, Black must keep the pieces on the board and stay busy, because in a pure endgame the pawn is only a weakness.

## How to Play the Position

White's plan is to blockade d4 with a knight and to exchange pieces. Black's plan is to avoid unnecessary exchanges, place the pieces on active squares, and look for ...d4 or an attack on the kingside. If you meet the structure from the other side in the Vienna, you will know what your opponent wants as well.
