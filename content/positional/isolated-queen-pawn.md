An isolated queen pawn cannot be defended by another pawn, and the square in front of it is an ideal home for an enemy knight. Those are its weaknesses. Yet the same pawn controls two central squares, frees the pieces behind it, and gives its owner open files. Whichever side has it, the plan is clear, and you will meet this structure from both sides in your repertoire.

## With the Pawn

Keep the pieces on, because active pieces are the compensation. Use e5 and c5 as landing spots, look for an attack on the king, and prepare the advance of the pawn when it can do damage.

## Against the Pawn

Blockade the square in front of it with a knight. Exchange pieces, since every trade makes the pawn weaker. Attack it with rooks in the endgame, where it can no longer be compensated by activity.

```choice
after: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 c5 4.exd5 exd5 5.Ngf3 Nc6 6.Bb5 Bd6 7.dxc5 Bxc5 8.O-O Nge7 9.Nb3 Bd6
question: Black has an isolated pawn on d5. Which move follows the plan against it?
answer: b3d4
option: b3d4 | The knight settles on the blockading square, directly in front of the pawn, and no pawn can drive it away.
option: g2g4 | A wild flank move in front of White's own king. The plan against an isolated pawn is calm and positional.
option: h2h4 | This does nothing about the pawn or the d4-square, and it loosens White's kingside.
```
