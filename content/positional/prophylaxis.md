Aron Nimzowitsch gave the idea its name, but every good player uses it. Prophylaxis means stopping your opponent's plan before it starts. The method is simple to describe, and it comes down to asking one question after every move: **what would they play if it were their turn again?**

Having found the answer, ask whether you can make that move less attractive without giving up your own plans.

## The Everyday Examples

- A timely h3 or ...h6 stops a pin on g4 or g5, and it also gives the king air on the back rank.
- A rook placed opposite the enemy queen makes the queen uncomfortable long before anything happens.
- A king stepped towards the centre before the ending begins saves a crucial tempo later.

## Do Not Overdo It

Prophylaxis exists to serve a plan. If you spend every move preventing things, you will end up with nothing to do yourself. The best prophylactic moves stop what the opponent wants and also help you with what you want.
