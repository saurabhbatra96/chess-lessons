The Carlsbad arises when White exchanges on d5 and Black recaptures with the e-pawn. It looks harmless, and White's most famous plan looks strange at first: advancing the pawns where White has fewer of them. That is the minority attack.

White's two pawns on a2 and b2 advance against Black's three on a7, b7 and c6. When the b-pawn reaches b5 and exchanges on c6, Black is left with a backward pawn on the half-open c-file, which White can then attack for the rest of the game.

## From the White Side

The usual method is to bring a rook to b1, then advance b4 and b5. The moves are slow, but Black's position has few natural pawn breaks to oppose them.

```choice
after: 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.cxd5 exd5 5.Bg5 c6 6.e3 Be7 7.Bd3 O-O 8.Qc2 Re8 9.Nf3 Nbd7 10.O-O Nf8
question: White has finished developing. Which of these moves begins a sound plan?
answer: a1b1
option: a1b1 | The rook supports the b-pawn's advance, starting the minority attack.
option: e3e4 | The central break is premature, because Black's pieces already bear down on e4 and the pawn cannot be maintained there.
option: g2g4 | A pawn lunge in front of White's own king, with no pieces ready to support it.
```

## From the Black Side

Black's counterplay relies on the piece activity that the open e-file provides: a knight on e4 and a rook behind it. Black can also place a knight on c4 once the white pawns have moved, since the b-pawn's advance leaves that square behind for good.
