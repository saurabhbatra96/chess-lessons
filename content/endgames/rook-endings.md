"All rook endings are drawn" is an old joke, and it has some truth in it. The side with the extra pawn usually needs more than the pawn to win. Three principles matter most in these endings.

## Put Rooks Behind Passed Pawns

Siegbert Tarrasch's rule still holds. A rook behind a passed pawn gains scope as the pawn advances, while a rook in front of the pawn loses scope with every step. It applies to your own passed pawns and to your opponent's.

```choice
fen: 6k1/5pp1/7p/P7/8/2r3P1/5P1P/3R2K1 w - - 0 1
question: White's a-pawn is passed. Which rook move follows Tarrasch's rule?
answer: d1a1
option: d1a1 | The rook stands behind the pawn and pushes it from behind. Black's rook will have to give up its activity to deal with it.
option: d1f1 | A passive rook, guarding the f-pawn. It does nothing to help the passed pawn.
```

## Activity Above All

A passive rook loses more endings than a missing pawn. If you must choose between defending a pawn passively and giving it up for an active rook, give up the pawn more often than instinct suggests.

## Cut Off the King

A rook on an open file can keep the enemy king away from the action. If you cut off the defending king by two or more files from your passed pawn, you often have a winning position without doing anything else.
