You have reached a won ending. What remains is to avoid giving it back. These endings are not difficult, but each contains one or two traps that catch out even experienced players.

## Beware of Stalemate

With a queen against a bare king, there are many ways to go wrong in the last few moves. Before you play any quiet queen move near a cornered king, check that the king still has a move.

## The Wrong Bishop

A bishop and a rook pawn cannot always win. If the corner where the pawn promotes is not the colour of your bishop, and the defending king gets there, the bishop can never drive it out. The position is a dead draw, however much extra material it may look like.

```board
fen: 7k/8/5K1P/8/8/3B4/8/8 w - - 0 1
caption: The light-squared bishop can never control h8, which is a dark square. White cannot force the black king out, and the game is drawn.
```

## Knights and Pawns

Knights are short-range pieces that struggle against passed pawns on opposite wings. Bishops prefer play on both sides of the board. Keep this in mind when you decide which minor piece to trade.
