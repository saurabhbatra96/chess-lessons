About half of all endgames contain rooks, and these two positions are the foundation of every rook ending. One tells you how to win, and the other tells you how to save a draw. Learn both until you can play them without thinking.

## The Lucena: Building a Bridge

White's pawn is on the seventh, with the king in front of it. The king wants to step out, but enemy checks drive it back. The solution is to bring the rook to the fourth rank, where it can interpose against those checks.

```line
side: w
depth: 20
start: 1K6/1P1k4/8/8/8/8/7r/2R5 w - - 0 1
caption: The Lucena position
moves: 1.Rd1+ Ke7 2.Rd4 Rh1 3.Kc7 Rc1+ 4.Kb6 Rb1+ 5.Kc6 Rc1+ 6.Kb5 Rb1+ 7.Rb4
note: 1.Rd1+ | The check drives the black king one file further from the pawn.
note: 2.Rd4 | The key move. The rook goes to the fourth rank to prepare the bridge.
note: 7.Rb4 | The bridge is built. The checks are over and the pawn will queen.
```

## The Philidor: Holding the Third Rank

When the attacking king has not yet reached the sixth rank, the defender has a simple, reliable drawing method. Keep your rook on the rank that the attacking king wants to reach. When the pawn finally advances, drop the rook to the far end of the board and check from behind.

```line
side: b
start: 4k3/R7/7r/3KP3/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1
caption: The Philidor position
moves: 1...Rb6 2.Rh7 Ra6 3.e6 Ra1 4.Kd6 Rd1+ 5.Ke5 Re1+ 6.Kf6 Rf1+
note: 1...Rb6 | The rook stays on the sixth rank, keeping the white king out.
note: 3...Ra1 | The pawn has advanced, so the king has no shelter, and the rook goes to the far end.
note: 4...Rd1+ | The checks begin, and White's king cannot hide from them.
```
