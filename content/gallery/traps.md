Everyone falls into a trap sooner or later. It is better to fall into these here, where it costs nothing. Each one below is played out as a short game: guess the winning side's moves, then step through to see how the mistake happened.

## The Englund Gambit

Against 1.d4, this dubious gambit sets a nasty trap. White's natural-looking bishop development on f4 walks straight into it.

```game
side: b
from: 3
caption: Englund Gambit trap
moves: 1.d4 e5 2.dxe5 Nc6 3.Nf3 Qe7 4.Bf4 Qb4+ 5.Bd2 Qxb2 6.Bc3 Bb4 7.Qd2 Bxc3 8.Qxc3 Qc1#
note: 4...Qb4+ | The queen forks the king and the pawn on b2.
note: 6...Bb4 | The bishop on c3 is pinned to the king.
note: 8...Qc1# | The queen has been lured away, and the back rank is undefended.
```

## The Stafford Gambit

Popular online, and it depends entirely on White taking the bait.

```game
side: b
from: 5
caption: Stafford Gambit trap
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 Nc6 4.Nxc6 dxc6 5.e5 Ne4 6.d3 Bc5 7.dxe4 Bxf2+ 8.Ke2 Bg4+
note: 6...Bc5 | Black ignores the attack on the knight and aims at f2.
note: 7...Bxf2+ | If 8.Kxf2, then ...Qxd1 wins the queen.
note: 8...Bg4+ | The queen on d1 is lost in any case.
```

## The Blackburne Shilling

```game
side: b
from: 3
caption: Blackburne Shilling Gambit
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nd4 4.Nxe5 Qg5 5.Nxf7 Qxg2 6.Rf1 Qxe4+ 7.Be2 Nf3#
note: 4...Qg5 | Hitting both the knight and the g2-pawn.
note: 7...Nf3# | A smothered mate: the white pieces have taken every square from their own king.
```

## The Lasker Trap

Worth knowing for your Queen's Gambit, since a similar gambit can be played against you.

```game
side: b
from: 3
caption: Albin Countergambit: the Lasker Trap
moves: 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 d4 4.e3 Bb4+ 5.Bd2 dxe3 6.Bxb4 exf2+ 7.Ke2 fxg1=N+
note: 6...exf2+ | The pawn captures with check.
note: 7...fxg1=N+ | The rare underpromotion: a knight gives check, where a queen could simply be captured.
```

## The Fried Liver

As White against the Two Knights, this one is real, and it rewards every careless reply.

```line
side: w
caption: 5...Nxd5 is the mistake; Black should have played 5...Na5
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxf7 Kxf7 7.Qf3+ Ke6 8.Nc3
note: 6.Nxf7 | The sacrifice drags the king into the open.
note: 8.Nc3 | The pinned knight on d5 is attacked again, and Black's king has no safe home.
```
