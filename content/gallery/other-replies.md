Not everyone answers 1.e4 with 1...e5, the Sicilian, the French or the Caro-Kann. Three other replies appear regularly at club level. None requires much study, as long as you know the first few moves and a sensible plan.

## The Scandinavian

Black's queen comes out early and can be chased. Develop with tempo.

```line
side: w
caption: 1...d5, the Scandinavian
moves: 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qa5 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 c6 6.Bc4
note: 3.Nc3 | Development that attacks the queen.
note: 6.Bc4 | White has a comfortable lead in development and a free hand in the centre.
```

## The Alekhine

Black's knight invites the white pawns forward so that it can attack them later. Do not overdo the chasing.

```line
side: w
caption: 1...Nf6, the Alekhine
moves: 1.e4 Nf6 2.e5 Nd5 3.d4 d6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Be2 e6 6.O-O
note: 4.Nf3 | Calm development. The Modern Variation needs little memory.
```

## The Pirc

Here your f-pawn feels at home again. The Austrian Attack is simply a bigger version of the plan you already know.

```line
side: w
caption: 1...d6, the Pirc and the Austrian Attack
moves: 1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6 4.f4 Bg7 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Bd3
note: 4.f4 | The f-pawn again, this time with a big centre behind it.
note: 6.Bd3 | White prepares castling and the e5 push.
```
