A gambit is a loan. You give up material now and expect repayment in time, space or attacking chances. The question to ask of any gambit is whether the repayment is real. The good news for the defender is that you do not need to know a refutation by heart. Usually it is enough to return the pawn at the right moment and develop.

## Declining with a Central Strike

A good rule is to answer a gambit by striking in the centre. Against the Danish, Black gives back the extra pawn and gets a comfortable game.

```line
side: b
caption: The Danish Gambit, met with 3...d5
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.c3 d5 4.exd5 Nf6 5.Nf3 Nxd5 6.Nxd4 Be7
note: 3...d5 | The central strike. Black refuses to spend time keeping the pawn.
note: 5...Nxd5 | Black's pieces come out easily and the game is equal.
```

## A Gambit You May Enjoy

The King's Gambit is the ancestor of your Vienna, and it has the same spirit. If Black keeps the extra pawn, White has the open f-file and a big centre.

```line
side: w
caption: The King's Gambit
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 d6 4.d4 g5 5.h4 g4 6.Ng1
note: 2.f4 | The oldest attacking idea in the book.
note: 5.h4 | White immediately challenges the pawn chain that defends f4.
note: 6.Ng1 | An odd-looking retreat, but Black's advanced pawns can become targets.
```

## Meeting the Budapest

This one can be played as Black against 1.d4. Black usually wins the pawn back, and the fight is about development.

```line
side: b
caption: The Budapest Gambit, from Black's side
moves: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 Ng4 4.Bf4 Nc6 5.Nf3 Bb4+ 6.Nbd2 Qe7 7.a3 Ngxe5
note: 3...Ng4 | The pawn on e5 is attacked at once.
note: 7...Ngxe5 | Material is restored, and Black's pieces are active.
```

## How to Tell

Ask three questions when you are offered a gambit. How many tempi will it cost me to keep the pawn? Can I give it back for something useful? And where will my king be safe? Those answers matter more than any line you might have memorised.
