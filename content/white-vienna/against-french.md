You play the French yourself, so you know how comfortable its main lines can be for Black. The King's Indian Attack sidesteps them. White makes a set of simple developing moves in the same order almost every game, and the plan never changes: push the e-pawn to e5, reroute the knight, and attack the king.

```line
side: w
caption: The standard set-up
moves: 1.e4 e6 2.d3 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6 4.Ngf3 c5 5.g3 Nc6 6.Bg2 Be7 7.O-O O-O 8.Re1 b5 9.e5 Nd7 10.Nf1
note: 2.d3 | A modest move with a clear idea: keep the e-pawn on the board.
note: 5.g3 | The bishop goes to g2, where it supports e4 and later e5.
note: 8.Re1 | The rook gets behind the e-pawn before it advances.
note: 9.e5 | The pawn claims space and drives the knight from the kingside.
note: 10.Nf1 | The knight heads for h2 or e3, then g4, joining the attack.
```

Black's counterplay comes on the queenside with ...b5 and ...a5. Do not stop to defend it unless you must. White's attack is on the king, and Black's is only on pawns.
