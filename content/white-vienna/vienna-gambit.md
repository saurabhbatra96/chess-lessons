The idea of this whole repertoire fits in one sentence: develop the queen's knight first, then push the f-pawn. In the Vienna, 2.Nc3 guards e4 and leaves the f-pawn free to advance on move three, something the usual 2.Nf3 does not allow.

Against a typical club player this is ideal. There is little theory to learn, the positions are open and attacking, and most opponents have spent far more time preparing for the Italian and the Ruy Lopez.

## The Main Line: 3...d5

Black's best reply is to strike back in the centre at once. White captures on e5 and gains a space-grabbing pawn, and Black's knight jumps to e4.

```line
side: w
caption: The main line, with 5.Nf3 and a quick d4
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.f4 d5 4.fxe5 Nxe4 5.Nf3 Be7 6.d4 O-O 7.Bd3 f5 8.exf6 Bxf6 9.O-O Nc6
note: 3.f4 | The point of the opening. White attacks e5 and opens the f-file.
note: 4.fxe5 | The pawn on e5 cramps Black and takes away f6 from the knight.
note: 5.Nf3 | Simple development. The knight also keeps an eye on e5 and heads for the attack.
note: 6.d4 | White owns the centre. Bd3 comes next, aiming at the kingside.
note: 8.exf6 | Taking en passant opens the f-file for White's rook.
note: 9.O-O | White has finished developing, with a half-open file and easy play.
```

## If Black Takes the Pawn

The capture on f4 looks natural but hands White a tempo. The e-pawn advances and hits the knight, which must retreat all the way home.

```line
side: w
caption: 3...exf4, punished by 4.e5
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.f4 exf4 4.e5 Ng8 5.Nf3 d6 6.d4 dxe5 7.Qe2 Be7 8.Qxe5 Nf6 9.Bxf4 O-O
note: 4.e5 | The knight has nowhere good to go.
note: 7.Qe2 | Regaining the pawn with the queen keeps Black's pieces tied down.
note: 9.Bxf4 | Material is level, and White's pieces are the better placed.
```

## If Black Stays Solid

Some players answer with the modest ...d6. There is no need to force matters: White develops and keeps the option of taking on e5 for later.

```line
side: w
caption: 3...d6, a quiet alternative
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.f4 d6 4.Nf3 Nc6 5.Bb5 Bd7 6.d3
note: 5.Bb5 | The bishop eyes the knight, which defends e5.
note: 6.d3 | White supports e4, and the f-pawn tension can wait.
```
