Not everyone will walk into 2...Nf6 and 3.f4. Against the other second moves, White plays the same way with a little more preparation: finish the kingside development first, and let the f-pawn advance when it is ready.

## Against 2...Nc6

Bring the bishop to c4, keep the centre with d3, and only then consider f4. Rushing the pawn here leaves the g1-a7 diagonal exposed.

```line
side: w
caption: 2...Nc6 3.Bc4, the solid set-up
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.d3 Bc5 5.Nf3 d6 6.Na4 Bb6 7.c3
note: 3.Bc4 | The bishop takes up its best diagonal and targets f7.
note: 4.d3 | The e-pawn is protected, so ...Nxe4 tricks lose their force.
note: 6.Na4 | Chasing the bishop gains the pair of bishops if Black allows the exchange.
```

## Against 2...Bc5

Black's early bishop allows a well-known tactical idea. Leave the piece on c5 alone, develop the knight, and be ready to play d4 at the right moment.

```line
side: w
caption: 2...Bc5 3.Nf3, with a trick up the sleeve
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Bc5 3.Nf3 d6 4.Na4 Bb6 5.Nxb6 axb6 6.d4
note: 4.Na4 | Going straight after the bishop.
note: 5.Nxb6 | White gets the two bishops, while Black has doubled pawns.
note: 6.d4 | With the bishop pair, White wants the position opened.
```

The trick appears if Black develops carelessly with 3...Nc6. White replies 4.Nxe5! Nxe5 5.d4, and the pawn fork wins the piece back with a strong centre.

```line
side: w
caption: 3...Nc6, and the fork trick
moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Bc5 3.Nf3 Nc6 4.Nxe5 Nxe5 5.d4 Bd6 6.dxe5 Bxe5
note: 4.Nxe5 | It looks like a blunder, but the d-pawn is about to arrive.
note: 5.d4 | A fork of the bishop and the knight.
note: 6.dxe5 | Material is level, and White has the centre.
```
