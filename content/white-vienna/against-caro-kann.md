Caro-Kann players are often after a calm game, and they tend to know their main lines well. The move 3.f3 denies them both. It supports e4 with the pawn you wanted to use anyway, and it leads quickly to positions that few of them have studied. It is often called the Fantasy Variation.

```line
side: w
caption: 3...dxe4, the critical reply
moves: 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.f3 dxe4 4.fxe4 e5 5.Nf3 exd4 6.Bc4
note: 3.f3 | The f-pawn again, this time in support.
note: 4.fxe4 | White has a big centre and the half-open f-file.
note: 6.Bc4 | White gives up the d-pawn for fast development and an attack on f7.
```

## A Solid Alternative

Many opponents will decline to open the game and play ...e6 instead. Develop naturally and keep the centre.

```line
side: w
caption: 3...e6
moves: 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.f3 e6 4.Nc3 Nf6 5.Bg5 Be7 6.e5 Nfd7 7.Bxe7 Qxe7 8.f4
note: 5.Bg5 | Pinning the knight increases the pressure on d5.
note: 6.e5 | Locking the centre gains space, as in the French.
note: 8.f4 | The f-pawn reaches its favourite square at last.
```
