The Sicilian is the most frequent reply you will meet, and its main lines need a great deal of study. The Grand Prix Attack avoids them with the now familiar recipe: knight to c3, then the f-pawn.

White aims at the kingside and pays little attention to Black's play on the other wing. Against a player who has memorised the Najdorf, it can feel like an ambush.

```line
side: w
caption: 2...Nc6 3.f4 g6, the most common set-up
moves: 1.e4 c5 2.Nc3 Nc6 3.f4 g6 4.Nf3 Bg7 5.Bb5 Nd4 6.O-O Nxb5 7.Nxb5 d6
note: 3.f4 | The same pawn, the same plan.
note: 5.Bb5 | The bishop bears down on the knight and prepares quick castling.
note: 6.O-O | White does not fear the exchange on b5, since the knight lands on an active square.
```

## Against ...e6 and ...d5

If Black prepares a central strike with ...e6, White develops and meets ...d5 calmly. A check on b5 is often useful to disturb Black's coordination.

```line
side: w
caption: 2...e6 3.f4 d5
moves: 1.e4 c5 2.Nc3 e6 3.f4 d5 4.Nf3 Nf6 5.Bb5+ Bd7 6.Bxd7+ Qxd7 7.e5
note: 5.Bb5+ | A useful check before Black can complete development.
note: 7.e5 | The pawn gains space and drives away the knight.
```

## Against ...d6

```line
side: w
caption: 2...d6 3.f4
moves: 1.e4 c5 2.Nc3 d6 3.f4 Nf6 4.Nf3 g6 5.Bc4 Bg7 6.O-O O-O 7.d3
note: 5.Bc4 | The bishop stares at f7, where the f-file will soon meet it.
note: 7.d3 | White is ready for Qe1 and Qh4, the standard route to the kingside.
```

Remember that plan. With the queen on h4, the f-pawn advancing to f5 and the bishop on c4, White's attack builds almost by itself.
