An attack is an exercise in arithmetic. Count the pieces you can bring to the enemy king, then count the pieces available to defend it. If you have more, and the lines are open or can be opened, then you have an attack. If you have one or two pieces on their own, you have only a hope.

## Before You Begin

- **Is the centre closed or under your control?** A flank attack can be refuted by a counterblow in the middle.
- **How many pieces are coming?** Bring them all before the critical moment, if you can.
- **Which lines can you open?** Pawn advances, exchanges and sacrifices all do this job.

## Sacrifices Are Investments

Sacrificing material for an attack requires concrete reasons. The Greek Gift, Bxh7+, is the most famous example, and it depends on specific conditions: a knight ready for g5, a queen ready for h5, and no black knight or bishop able to deal with them. Do not play it from memory. Check each reply in the position in front of you, since the pattern often fails by a single square.
