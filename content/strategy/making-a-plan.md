"I had no idea what to do" is the most common complaint after a quiet game. A plan does not need to be long or brilliant. It only needs to answer one question better than your opponent's does: what would improve my position most?

## Three Questions

Ask these in order whenever nothing tactical is happening.

- **Which is my worst piece?** Improving it is rarely a wasted move.
- **Which is my opponent's weakest point?** A loose pawn, a weak square, or a king short of defenders can give every later move a purpose.
- **Which pawn move opens the game for my pieces?** Pawn breaks are how a closed position gets changed.

The third question is the one that club players most often overlook. Pieces gather behind the pawns, but nothing changes until a pawn moves.

```choice
fen: r1bq1rk1/pp3ppp/2nbpn2/3p4/3P4/2NBPN2/PP3PPP/R1BQ1RK1 w - - 0 9
question: A symmetrical position with both sides developed. Which pawn move gives White's pieces a purpose?
answer: e3e4
option: e3e4 | The central break. It frees the bishop on c1, opens lines for the other pieces, and is the natural way to change a position like this one.
option: b2b4 | The pawn is simply lost. The bishop on d6 takes it, and the pawn break has only given Black a free pawn.
option: g2g4 | A flank advance in front of White's own king, while the centre is still undecided, creates weaknesses that Black can use.
```

## A Plan Is Only a Direction

You do not need to see the end of the road. Decide where your pieces belong, and start moving them there. If your opponent changes the position, ask the three questions again.
